If your business still uses the ATO's free clearing house to pay super, I need to get your attention for the next few minutes, because it stops working for you on 1 July 2026. That's the date Payday Super starts, and it's not a small tweak to how super gets paid, it's a complete change to the timing, the reporting, and the penalty structure behind every super payment your business makes. Today I want to walk through a practical readiness checklist that works no matter what payroll software you're on.

[SECTION 1 - WHAT PAYDAY SUPER ACTUALLY CHANGES]
Right now, employers pay superannuation guarantee quarterly, with up to 28 days after each quarter to get it done. From 1 July 2026, that changes completely. Super has to be paid in line with each payday instead, and it generally needs to be received by the employee's fund within 7 business days after that payday. For a weekly payroll, that's the difference between four super payments a year and fifty-two. The obligation itself isn't bigger, but the window to catch a mistake before it compounds gets a lot smaller.

[SECTION 2 - QUALIFYING EARNINGS AND THE RATE]
Under the new system, the ATO uses the term qualifying earnings to describe what super guarantee is calculated on, things like ordinary time earnings, commissions, and salary sacrificed super. The rate applied is 12%, which has been in place since 1 July 2025. What matters here isn't the rate, it's making sure every pay item in your payroll system is mapped correctly against that qualifying earnings definition, because under the old quarterly system a mapping error got caught, at worst, four times a year. Under Payday Super, it gets repeated every single payday until somebody finds it.

[SECTION 3 - THE NEW PENALTY STRUCTURE]
And the cost of getting it wrong is going up too. If a super payment doesn't land within that 7 business day window, the redesigned Superannuation Guarantee Charge kicks in. It's still not tax deductible, and now it carries an administrative uplift amount on top of the unpaid super and interest, plus penalties of 25 to 50 percent of the unpaid charge depending on your compliance history. There's some relief built in if you've got a clean record and self-report early, but the clear message from the ATO is that Payday Super compliance failures get found, and cost more, faster than they used to.

[SECTION 4 - THE CLEARING HOUSE DEADLINE]
Here's the one item on this checklist that genuinely cannot wait. The ATO has confirmed the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House won't accept any payments made on or after 1 July 2026. If that's what your business currently uses, you need an alternative, either a clearing house built into your payroll platform or a separate commercial one, set up and tested against a real pay run before the cutover, not during it. Leave this to the last few weeks and you risk a gap where you can pay wages but genuinely can't pay super, which is exactly the scenario the new penalty structure is built to catch.

[SECTION 5 - THE FULL CHECKLIST]
So pulling it together, here's what to work through. Confirm your payroll software vendor has Payday Super functionality built and tested. Sort out your clearing house now, not in June. Audit every employee's super fund details for accuracy. Map every pay item against the qualifying earnings definition. Model what paying super every cycle instead of every quarter does to your cash flow. Confirm your Single Touch Payroll reporting will handle qualifying earnings and the super liability correctly from day one. Check any closely held employees or irregular pay arrangements individually, they carry their own timing rules. And run a full test pay cycle before you go live for real.

[SECTION 6 - WHAT'S DIFFERENT FOR NDIS, MEDICAL AND ALLIED HEALTH]
If you're in the NDIS, medical, or allied health space specifically, a couple of things are worth double checking. NDIS providers running large casual workforces under the SCHADS Award tend to have more allowances and penalty rates to check against qualifying earnings. Medical practices often have closely held employees, principals paid irregularly, who need individual attention rather than the default payroll setup. And allied health clinics mixing employed and contracted practitioners should double check those contractor arrangements genuinely sit outside the super guarantee rules, rather than assuming the contract label settles it.
