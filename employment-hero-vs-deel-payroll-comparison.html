Employment Hero vs Deel vs Payoneer: Which Fits NDIS & Allied Health Payroll?

Quick answer

Employment Hero is built for Australian award compliance, onboarding and native payroll, and is the stronger fit for NDIS and allied health providers employing staff under the SCHADS Award or another modern award. Deel is built for hiring and paying contractors and employees in other countries as an employer of record. Payoneer is a cross-border payments platform, useful for sending or receiving international payments, but it is not a payroll or HR system at all. Most Australian NDIS and allied health providers only need one of these, Employment Hero, the other two matter only if part of the workforce or the payment flow sits outside Australia.

Why Payroll Compliance Now Matters More

The regulatory environment around allied health and NDIS payroll has tightened on several fronts at once. The Fair Work Ombudsman treats intentional underpayment as a criminal matter, not just a civil one, since the start of 2025. SCHADS Award classification, sleepover allowances, broken shifts and travel time are exactly the kind of detail generic payroll software flattens into ordinary hours without anyone noticing. And the NDIA can review claims well after they've been paid under its Provider Payment Assurance Program, which means the payroll and rostering data behind a claim needs to hold up months later, not just at the time it was processed. Software that's built around these realities, rather than adapted to them after the fact, is a genuinely different starting point.

Where Employment Hero Excels

Employment Hero is an Australian-built HR and payroll platform, and it shows in how it handles award interpretation, onboarding and compliance documentation, the areas that matter most for a growing NDIS or allied health provider.

Onboarding at volume. Employment Hero's onboarding workflows let a provider build a standard new-starter flow once, contract, induction, policy acknowledgement, compliance and screening documents, then run every new hire through it automatically. For a provider adding support workers steadily rather than one at a time, particularly organisations running 100 or more staff, this is where Employment Hero separates itself. The admin burden of onboarding stops scaling linearly with headcount, because the flow doesn't need to be rebuilt or manually chased for every new starter.

Integrates with Xero and MYOB. Employment Hero can run as a full native Australian payroll, or it can sit on top as the HR, onboarding and time and attendance layer while the actual pay run and ledger stay in Xero or MYOB. That flexibility matters for providers who already have an established Xero file and bookkeeping process and don't want to migrate the ledger itself, just fix what sits above it.

Time and rostering data. Employment Hero has its own time and attendance module, and connects to rostering and timesheet data through its open API and standard integration tooling, which is how the actual worked hours, shift types and locations that live in rostering software make it into a payroll-ready format. Whichever rostering system a provider uses day to day, the practical question is always whether that data is being mapped into the correct pay item on the way through, not just imported as a flat number of hours.

Where Deel Fits

Deel solves a different problem. It's built for hiring, paying and staying compliant with employees and contractors in other countries, employer of record arrangements, international contractor payments, and multi-country compliance, at speed. For an Australian NDIS or allied health provider whose whole team is employed locally under the SCHADS Award or another Australian modern award, Deel isn't solving a problem that exists. It becomes genuinely useful the moment a provider also engages staff outside Australia, a remote clinician, an offshore admin contractor, or a specialist consulted internationally, running alongside the local Australian team rather than replacing local payroll.

If that describes your organisation, Deel is worth a look specifically for the international side of your workforce, not as a substitute for Australian award-compliant payroll.

Where Payoneer Fits

Payoneer is a different category again, it's a cross-border payments platform, not a payroll or HR system. It doesn't handle award classification, onboarding, superannuation or STP reporting at all. Where it's genuinely useful is moving money internationally, paying an overseas consultant, supplier or specialist, or receiving funds from an overseas client or funding source. For a provider whose entire workforce and payment flow sits inside Australia, Payoneer has no real role to play. For one with an occasional need to send or receive international payments alongside a fully Australian-employed team, Payoneer covers that specific gap without pretending to be a payroll system.

Which One Should You Actually Use

If every support worker or clinician on your books is employed inside Australia, Employment Hero is the tool built for that job, particularly once onboarding volume starts to matter. If you also engage staff or contractors overseas alongside your Australian team, Deel is worth adding for that specific slice of the workforce, run alongside Employment Hero or Xero or MYOB for the Australian side, not instead of it. If your only international need is moving money, paying an overseas supplier or receiving an overseas payment, rather than employing someone, Payoneer covers that without the overhead of a full employer-of-record platform. The software choice is only ever half the answer though, the SCHADS classification, allowance mapping and Xero or MYOB reconciliation behind whichever platform you pick is where the actual compliance risk sits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should an NDIS or allied health provider use Employment Hero or Deel?

For a provider employing support workers or clinicians inside Australia under the SCHADS Award or another modern award, Employment Hero is the closer fit, it is built around Australian award compliance, onboarding and native payroll. Deel is built for hiring and paying contractors and employees in other countries. A provider only needs Deel if it also employs or contracts staff outside Australia, alongside its local NDIS or allied health team.

Does Employment Hero work alongside Xero or MYOB?

Yes. Employment Hero can run its own native Australian payroll, or it can be used purely for HR, onboarding and time and attendance while payroll data is synced across to Xero or MYOB. Which setup makes sense depends on whether a provider wants one system for everything or wants to keep Xero as the ledger of record.

Can Deel handle SCHADS Award payroll?

No, not in any meaningful sense. Deel is built for global contractor payments and international employer of record arrangements, not Australian modern award interpretation. SCHADS Award classification, sleepover and broken shift allowances need a payroll setup built around Australian award rules, which is Employment Hero's or a dedicated Australian payroll provider's territory, not Deel's.

How does Employment Hero handle onboarding for a larger support worker team?

Employment Hero's onboarding workflows let a provider build a standard new-starter flow, contract, induction, policy acknowledgement, compliance documents, once, then run every new hire through it automatically. For a provider bringing on support workers at volume, particularly organisations running 100 or more staff, that removes a large share of the manual admin that would otherwise scale linearly with headcount.

Where does Payoneer fit for an NDIS or allied health provider?

Payoneer is a cross-border payments platform, not a payroll or HR system. It is useful if a provider needs to receive payments from or send payments to overseas parties, an international consultant, a supplier, or a client outside Australia, but it does not handle SCHADS Award classification, onboarding, superannuation or Single Touch Payroll reporting. It solves a payments problem, not a payroll compliance problem.
