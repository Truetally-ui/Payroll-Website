[HOOK]
If you've ever looked at your Single Touch Payroll report and wondered why your gross wages figure looks different to what it used to, or why an old employee's final pay is still causing an error message, there's a good chance the answer is STP Phase 2. It's been mandatory for most Australian employers for a while now, but it's still one of the most common sources of payroll errors we come across, especially for NDIS providers and allied health clinics running more complex award structures. So today I want to walk through what STP Phase 2 actually requires, and where the setup usually goes wrong.

[SECTION 1 — WHAT STP PHASE 2 ACTUALLY IS]
Single Touch Payroll Phase 2 is an expansion of the original STP reporting standard. Under the original version, all you had to report each pay run was one combined gross wages figure, the tax withheld, and your super liability. Phase 2 asks for a lot more detail, and it asks for it every single pay event, not as a separate form somewhere else.

The ATO built it this way for a reason. In exchange for more detailed reporting up front, a few other obligations disappear. You no longer need to give a departing employee a separate employment separation certificate, and new employees no longer need to lodge a TFN declaration directly with the ATO, because that information is now captured automatically through your STP report. So it's not purely extra admin, some of it replaces steps you used to have to do manually.

[SECTION 2 — DISAGGREGATION OF GROSS]
The biggest single change is something the ATO calls disaggregation of gross. Instead of reporting one number for gross wages, you now need to break that number into its component parts. Things like overtime, bonuses and commissions, director's fees, salary sacrifice amounts, and paid leave all need to be reported as their own separate line items, with whatever is left over reported as the residual gross amount.

If your business runs a simple salaried pay structure, this is a fairly small adjustment. But if you're running the SCHADS Award, which is common across NDIS and allied health providers, a single pay run might include base rates, multiple allowances, weekend and public holiday penalty rates, sleepover payments, and leave loading. Every one of those needs to be mapped correctly to its own STP Phase 2 category. That's a much bigger job, and it's exactly where we see setups go only half right.

[SECTION 3 — INCOME TYPES AND CESSATION REASONS]
On top of disaggregation, every payment you make also needs an income type attached to it. For most employees that's simply salary and wages. But there are other income types for specific situations, working holiday makers, closely held payees, which often comes up in family-run medical practices, and foreign employment income. Get the income type wrong and you can end up with an employee being taxed incorrectly, or a payment being misreported to Services Australia.

The other piece that trips people up is cessation reporting. When someone's employment ends, STP Phase 2 requires you to record a cessation reason, using one of the ATO's set categories, voluntary cessation, ill health, contract cessation, redundancy, or transfer. According to the ATO's own published guidance, leaving this blank, or forgetting to include it on the final pay event, is one of the most common employer errors they see.

[SECTION 4 — THE MISTAKES WE SEE MOST]
So to make this concrete, here's the shortlist of what actually goes wrong in practice. Missing cessation dates and reasons. Pay categories accidentally marked as not reportable, when they should be flowing through to the ATO. Year-to-date figures losing continuity when a business transitions from Phase 1 to Phase 2 partway through a financial year. Country codes entered as "not applicable" when the ATO specifically requires an actual country code. And probably the most common one for our clients, allowances that get lumped into a general gross figure instead of being itemised the way Phase 2 requires.

None of these are exotic problems. They're the kind of thing that happens when a payroll system gets set up once, mostly correctly, and then nobody goes back to check it against every pay category as awards update and new allowances get added.

[SECTION 5 — WHY IT MATTERS AND WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT]
The reason this is worth getting right isn't just tidiness. Because STP Phase 2 reports go to the ATO with every single pay run, a mapping error doesn't sit quietly in the background the way an old quarterly reporting mistake might have. It shows up in your year-to-date figures straight away, and it keeps compounding every pay cycle until someone catches it, usually at end of financial year finalisation, which is the worst possible time to discover it.

The most reliable fix is to actually sit down with the ATO's employer STP Phase 2 checklist and go through your real pay categories against it, rather than assuming your payroll software got it all right by default. For businesses on complex award structures, we'd usually build that into a broader payroll review rather than treating it as a one-off tick and flick, because these things drift over time.
