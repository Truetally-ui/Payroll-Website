[SECTION 1 - WHAT THE FAIR WORK COMMISSION ACTUALLY DECIDED]
On the 1st of June 2026, the Fair Work Commission's Full Bench handed down a landmark decision in the gender-based undervaluation review of the SCHADS Award. The Commission found the existing classification structure and wage rates had been affected by longstanding undervaluation of the work performed across the sector, mostly done by women. The decision introduces a brand new integrated classification structure that will eventually replace the current Schedules B, C, E and F, resets minimum wage rates, and removes the existing Equal Remuneration Order. But here's the key thing for payroll: this transition doesn't happen on one date for everyone.

[SECTION 2 - THE 4.75% INCREASE FROM 1 JULY 2026]
Separate from that classification restructure, the Fair Work Commission's Annual Wage Review confirmed a 4.75% increase to all SCHADS Award minimum rates, effective from the first full pay period on or after the 1st of July 2026. This one's straightforward, it applies across the whole award, home care, disability support, family and community services, social and community services, regardless of which schedule an employee falls under. If you only update one number in your payroll system this year, this is the one that touches everybody.

[SECTION 3 - THE SCHEDULE E INCREASE FROM 1 OCTOBER 2026]
The second increase is narrower but a lot bigger. As part of that same 1 June 2026 decision, Schedule E employees, that's Home Care Employees, Disability Care, get a further interim increase of around 15% to their minimum rates, from the first full pay period on or after the 1st of October 2026. The exact figure varies a bit by classification level, so check the actual pay guide rather than assuming a flat number. And this one stacks on top of the July increase, it doesn't replace it, so Schedule E employees are getting two pay rises in the same calendar year.

[SECTION 4 - WHAT ABOUT SCHEDULE B AND MOST NDIS SUPPORT WORKERS]
Here's where a lot of NDIS providers get caught out. Most NDIS support workers providing disability support outside a home care setting sit under Schedule B, not Schedule E. Schedule B employees still get the 4.75% July 2026 increase like everyone else, but not the bigger interim rise. Apply the Schedule E percentage to a Schedule B workforce and you've either created an underpayment risk or budgeted for a cost increase that was never coming this year.

[SECTION 5 - THE NDIS PRICING FLOW-ON]
And if you're an NDIS provider, this doesn't stop at payroll. The NDIS pricing arrangements build the SCHADS Award's labour cost into support item price limits, so a wage increase is meant to flow through to an updated price limit. Before you assume a wage increase is covered by what you're billing, check the relevant NDIS price limit has actually been updated, and that any existing service agreements have been reviewed with participants. Otherwise a fully compliant wage rise quietly turns into a margin problem.
