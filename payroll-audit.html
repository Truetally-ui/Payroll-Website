Where audits actually start

Payroll rarely breaks all at once.

It drifts. A classification set correctly on day one never gets reviewed as a role grows. A rostering system changes and the mapping into Xero doesn't quite keep up. An award update lands and nobody's certain whether it was actually applied. None of it looks urgent in any single pay run, until it's several years of quiet exposure sitting on the books, discovered by an employee, an accountant, or Fair Work, rather than by you.

When we recommend a payroll audit

Eight signs it's time to check

01

It's Been Over 12 Months Since the Last Review

Payroll isn't a set-and-forget system. A classification review, super reconciliation and STP check every 12 to 24 months catches drift before it compounds.

02

You've Had Turnover in Payroll or Bookkeeping

Whoever set up your payroll rules may not be the person maintaining them now. Handover gaps are one of the most common places award conditions quietly stop being applied.

03

You've Changed Rostering or Timesheet Software

Moving between Shiftcare, Brevity, Careview, Deputy, Employment Hero, Tanda or similar platforms is exactly when shift types stop mapping to the correct Xero pay item without anyone noticing.

04

An Award or Classification Has Changed

A Fair Work Commission determination or annual wage review, like the SCHADS Award changes taking effect from October 2026, is a direct trigger to confirm payroll actually reflects the new rules from the correct date.

05

A Staff Member Has Raised a Pay Query

One question about a pay run is often a signal, not an isolated event. It's worth checking whether the same issue affects other staff on the same classification or shift pattern.

06

You're Preparing for a Sale or Investment

Payroll compliance is a standard part of financial due diligence. An audit before you're in a transaction, not during one, gives you time to fix issues on your own terms.

07

Fair Work Has Made Contact

A compliance notice, a request for records, or any correspondence from the Fair Work Ombudsman is not the time to guess. An audit tells you exactly where you stand before you respond.

08

You've Grown Past a Handful of Employees

Payroll set up for two or three staff rarely scales cleanly to fifteen. Growth is when classification variety, shift complexity and award interactions genuinely increase.

Why this actually matters

This isn't a hypothetical risk

$2.05 Million in One Case

A Melbourne disability provider signed an Enforceable Undertaking with the Fair Work Ombudsman after rectifying $2.05 million in underpayments, including interest and super, to 1,389 current and former staff, underpaid over a period of six years before anyone caught it.

Multi-Year Federal Court Litigation

Providers across sectors have spent years in Federal Court over a single disputed allowance or unresolved Compliance Notice. Even those who ultimately prevail absorb years of legal cost and uncertainty getting there.

36% of Providers Audited, Not Compliant

A 2021 Fair Work Ombudsman audit of NDIS providers found 36% had incorrectly applied penalty rates, casual loadings, overtime or allowances. This pattern isn't unique to one sector, it's close to a coin flip wherever award interpretation is complex.

Underpayment Is Now a Criminal Offence

Since January 2025, intentional underpayment carries criminal penalties in Australia, up to 10 years imprisonment for individuals, and for companies a fine of at least $7.825 million or three times the underpayment amount, whichever is larger.

What we check

Four things a payroll configuration review covers

This is a system-level review of how your payroll is set up and configured, not a legal or industrial relations determination. If a review surfaces a genuine classification or underpayment question, we'll tell you plainly and point you to a workplace lawyer or the Fair Work Ombudsman for that specific call, that's their expertise, not ours.

01

Pay Item & Rate Setup Review

Every pay item in your Xero file checked against the award rates and classifications you've told us apply, confirming the setup is current and configured correctly, the single most common source of quiet drift as a role or roster changes.

02

Super, Payday Super & STP Reconciliation

Superannuation guarantee timing checked against Payday Super requirements, and Single Touch Payroll reporting reconciled against actual pay runs, so nothing's been quietly missed or filed late.

03

Pay Item to Source Data Reconciliation

Pay run outputs reconciled against the rostering or timesheet system behind them, Shiftcare, Brevity, Careview, Deputy, Employment Hero, Tanda or similar, to confirm shift types actually mapped to the correct pay item rather than defaulting to ordinary hours.

04

Configuration Fix & Handover Plan

Where a setup gap is found, a clear written plan showing exactly which pay items, rates or mappings need correcting in Xero, so pay runs going forward are accurate. If the gap points to a genuine back-pay question, that's flagged clearly as a matter to take to a workplace lawyer or the Fair Work Ombudsman, we fix the system, we don't make the legal call.
