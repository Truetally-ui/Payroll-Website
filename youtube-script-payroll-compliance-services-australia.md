"Payroll compliance services" is one of those phrases that gets used to describe about five different things, an outsourced payroll function, a one-off audit, some advisory work on a tricky Award question. So today I want to actually pin down what it means, why the stakes around it changed pretty dramatically in the last couple of years, and what to look for if you're deciding whether to bring in a provider.

[SECTION 1 — WHAT IT ACTUALLY COVERS]
Start with the basic distinction, because it matters more than it sounds like it should. Payroll processing is the mechanical part, calculating what someone's owed, generating the payslip, making the payment, on whatever cycle you run. Payroll compliance is what sits around that process, checking that what's being processed is actually correct. That means Single Touch Payroll Phase 2 reporting to the ATO is coded properly, superannuation guarantee is calculated on the right base and paid on time, every employee sits under the correct Award or agreement classification, and your records are being kept the way the Fair Work Regulations actually require. You can run payroll perfectly smoothly, on time, every fortnight, for years, and still be non-compliant the entire time if nobody's checking those things against current law.

[SECTION 2 — WHY THE STAKES CHANGED IN 2025]
Here's the part that's genuinely shifted the landscape. From the first of January 2025, under changes to the Fair Work Act, intentionally underpaying staff became a criminal offence. Not just a civil penalty, a criminal one. We're talking about individuals facing up to ten years in prison and fines currently up to one point six five million dollars, and companies facing fines currently up to eight point two five million dollars. Honest mistakes are carved out of that, the offence requires intent, but the civil penalties for non-intentional underpayment went up at the same time too, currently up to four hundred and ninety five thousand dollars per contravention for a company, or three times the underpayment amount, whichever is bigger. The Fair Work Ombudsman has also put out a voluntary small business wage compliance code, which genuinely helps if you follow it properly, but it doesn't remove the obligation to actually pay people correctly in the first place. So the honest summary is, the cost of getting payroll wrong went from "fix it and back-pay" to "potentially explain yourself to a criminal investigator," and that's exactly why more businesses are treating payroll compliance as its own dedicated function now instead of something that just happens inside bookkeeping.

[SECTION 3 — THE FIVE THINGS A REAL COMPLIANCE SERVICE CHECKS]
So what does a genuine payroll compliance service actually check, on an ongoing basis? Five things, roughly. STP Phase 2 reporting, making sure income types and pay components are coded correctly for every lodgement. Superannuation guarantee, calculated on the right ordinary time earnings figure and paid on time, which matters even more with Payday Super moving super to a per pay cycle payment from the first of July twenty twenty six. Award or agreement classification, making sure every employee is actually sitting at the correct level for the work they do, not just the level someone assigned them on day one. Record-keeping, kept in the specific form the Fair Work Regulations require, for at least seven years. And leave accrual, making sure annual leave, personal leave and long service leave are tracking correctly against each employee's actual entitlement.

[SECTION 4 — WHO'S ACTUALLY ALLOWED TO DO THIS WORK]
This is the bit people skip over. Advising on or managing the GST, PAYG withholding, or superannuation guarantee side of payroll, for a fee, is regulated BAS service work under the Tax Practitioners Board. That means whoever's doing it needs to be a registered BAS agent, or working under the supervision of one. Registration isn't a rubber stamp either, it requires a Certificate IV in accounting or bookkeeping or higher, a set number of hours of supervised experience, ongoing professional education that has to cover current ATO obligations, and professional indemnity insurance. You can actually check anyone's registration on the Tax Practitioners Board's public register before you engage them, and honestly, you should.

If the answer to all of that is basically "we just run the numbers you send us," that's a processing service, not a compliance service, whatever the marketing says.
