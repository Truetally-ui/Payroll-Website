Where it actually breaks

The roster is right. The payroll often isn't.

Most NDIS providers roster carefully in Shiftcare or similar software, real shifts, real locations, real client visits. The breakdown happens in the handoff to payroll: a sleepover shift imports as eight hours of ordinary time, a genuine broken shift gets paid as continuous hours, and a classification set correctly two years ago never gets revisited as someone's responsibilities grew. None of it looks wrong in any single pay run. It compounds into a wage claim or a Fair Work Ombudsman audit before anyone catches it.

Why this actually matters

This isn't a hypothetical risk

$2.05 Million in One Case

A Melbourne disability provider signed an Enforceable Undertaking with the Fair Work Ombudsman after rectifying $2.05 million in underpayments, including interest and super, to 1,389 current and former support workers, most of them casual, underpaid over a period of six years before anyone caught it.

Two Years in Federal Court Over One Allowance

A national SCHADS-covered provider spent close to two years in Federal Court litigation over a single disputed shift loading tied to sleepover shifts. Even providers who ultimately prevail on the specific point still absorb years of legal cost and uncertainty getting there.

36% of Providers Audited, Not Compliant

A 2021 Fair Work Ombudsman audit found 36% of NDIS providers reviewed had incorrectly applied penalty rates, casual loadings, overtime, broken shift and high-intensity support allowances. This isn't a rare failure mode, it's close to a coin flip.

Underpayment Is Now a Criminal Offence

Since January 2025, intentional underpayment carries criminal penalties in Australia, up to 10 years imprisonment for individuals, and for companies a fine of at least $7.825 million or three times the underpayment amount, whichever is larger. Fair Work also secured a record $21.2 million in court-ordered penalties in 2023-24 alone.

Straight answers

Providers ask us…

The Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services (SCHADS) Award sets classification levels, allowances and penalty rates for most NDIS support workers, including sleepover shifts, broken shifts and travel time between clients. Standard payroll software isn't configured for these rules by default, and generic payroll providers routinely process them as ordinary hours.

Sleepover shifts paid as a full night of hourly wages instead of the correct allowance, classification levels never reviewed as a support worker's role grows, and broken shift or travel time simply not being captured because the rostering data behind it isn't structured to catch it.

We map each shift type recorded in Shiftcare, or similar rostering software, to the correct Xero pay item, so sleepover, broken shift, travel time and standard hours flow through to payroll as what they actually were, not flattened into ordinary hours during import.

We run internal CPD sessions specifically on SCHADS and disability sector payroll, tracking Fair Work Commission determinations and annual wage reviews, so classification and allowance changes are reflected in your payroll from the pay cycle they take effect.
