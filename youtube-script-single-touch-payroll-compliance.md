[HOOK]
If your business has had Single Touch Payroll running for a few years now, it's easy to assume STP is a solved problem, the software's connected, pay runs go through, job done. But there's a difference between having STP set up and actually being STP compliant, and that difference matters a lot more than it used to. The ATO ran a pretty light-touch, transitional approach to STP penalties for years while everyone adjusted. That grace period ended in 2025. So today I want to walk through what STP compliance actually requires, separate from the technical setup, and what's changed about how seriously the ATO is now enforcing it.

[SECTION 1 - SETUP VERSUS COMPLIANCE]
Let's start by separating two things people often talk about as if they're the same. STP Phase 2 setup is about whether your payroll software correctly codes every payment, every income type, every cessation reason, the technical configuration side of things. STP compliance is different. It's about whether the reporting built on top of that setup is actually happening the way the ATO's rules require, on time, accurately, with errors fixed when they're found.

Here's the thing, you can have a technically perfect STP Phase 2 setup and still not be compliant. If pay events go in late, if the end of year finalisation gets missed, if an error sits there uncorrected for months, none of that is a setup problem. That's a compliance problem, and it's the one that actually attracts penalties.

[SECTION 2 - THE DEADLINES THAT ACTUALLY MATTER]
So what are the deadlines? First, pay event reporting. Under the ATO's rules, a pay event needs to be reported on or before payday, meaning the date the payment instruction goes to the bank, or the date you intend the money to land in the employee's account. That's every single pay cycle, weekly, fortnightly, monthly, not some periodic catch-up the way older reporting used to work.

Second, and this is the big one most people know about but still get wrong, end of year finalisation. Most employers need to make their finalisation declaration by 14 July, confirming the year's STP data is complete and accurate. If you use a registered tax or BAS agent, or you're dealing with closely held payees, there's sometimes access to a later deadline of 30 September, but that's the exception, not the default. And finalisation isn't just an internal admin task, it's literally the trigger that tells your employees their income statement is ready to use for their own tax return. Miss it, and you're not just risking a penalty, you're holding up your staff's tax returns.

[SECTION 3 - FIXING ERRORS THE RIGHT WAY]
Now, mistakes happen. An allowance gets mapped wrong, a pay event goes in a day late, a termination gets reported without a cessation reason. The ATO generally gives you fourteen days from the original pay event to correct an error without penalty, just submit an update or amended pay event through your software as soon as you spot it.

Here's why that window matters. Because STP data flows to the ATO in close to real time, an error that doesn't get corrected doesn't just sit there quietly. It compounds into every single pay event's year-to-date figures until someone catches it, usually at the worst possible time, which is finalisation. The ATO has actually been pretty clear that penalties are far more likely for errors that are left uncorrected, large, deliberate, or repeated, than for a genuine one-off mistake that gets fixed quickly. So the real risk isn't making an error. It's not fixing it.

[SECTION 4 - WHY THE STAKES CHANGED IN 2025]
This is the part that's genuinely new. For the first several years of STP, the ATO ran a mostly transitional approach to penalties, understandably, everyone was adjusting to a new system. That transitional period is over. The ATO has published new guidance on penalties for STP non-compliance, and released a draft practice statement, PS LA 2026 slash D2, setting out exactly how it plans to apply failure to lodge penalties to STP reporting specifically.

And those penalties aren't trivial. The base calculation is one penalty unit for every 28 days, or part of one, a report is overdue, up to a maximum of five penalty units, doubled for a medium withholder, multiplied by five for a large one. And the penalty unit itself just went up, from $330 to $364 from the first of July 2026. So the dollar value of getting this wrong has increased at the same time the ATO has gotten more serious about actually enforcing it.

[SECTION 5 - WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO ABOUT IT]
So practically, what does staying compliant look like? Build checks into the pay cycle itself rather than only looking at STP once a year. Confirm each pay event actually lodged on or before payday, not just that it was submitted eventually. Review any error-flagged events immediately, not at finalisation time. And treat 14 July as a checkpoint you're prepared for across the whole year, not a deadline you scramble toward in June. If you're running a more complex pay structure, SCHADS Award shifts, multiple allowances, a mix of employees and contractors, this is genuinely easier to sustain with dedicated payroll support than as something squeezed in around everything else.
